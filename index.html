How this model works

1. Your input percentages are the target vote shares across the full loaded test area.
2. The supplied Duchy previous-election results create the regional political baseline.
3. Age, urbanisation, income, education, housing, deprivation, family and tags alter each seat locally.
4. Multi-category interactions are also applied — for example, old + agricultural + high-income is more favourable to IMP than any of those traits alone, while low-income + high-deprivation is more strongly favourable to mainstream SDA than fringe VRK.
5. The model then re-balances all constituency estimates so the aggregate vote closely matches your input.

This means stronger demographic effects change where a party wins without simply inflating its national vote.

Candidate eligibility: Pamialukia Prata (PLF) only stands in PAM constituencies. Its score is forced to 0 outside Pamialukia, so it cannot win VRL, RBN or EBN seats.

Majority labels are based on the largest party's seat total relative to the majority threshold: within 20% below = No working majority; more than 20% below = Hung; 0–10% above = Slim majority; 10–50% above = Working majority; over 50% above = Supermajority.

Export your Affinity map as SVG. For automatic filling, name each closed constituency Curve with its constituency code, e.g. PAM1-1, VRL2-4, RBN1-3, or later KAN-3-5. The code may be on the SVG shape itself or on a parent group. Border protection: some exporters draw a constituency's border as its own filled black shape (no stroke at all) layered next to the white interior shape, rather than using an SVG stroke. The simulator only paints child shapes that already declare an explicit fill; shapes with no fill declared are treated as border/outline art and are never touched. Any shape that does use a real stroke additionally has its exact original stroke snapshotted and restored after filling, as a second layer of protection.
